# Ninja Worm! — design notes

## The pitch

One mechanic, deep: the **ninja rope**. You are a worm with a grappling hook and a headband. Fire,
swing, wrap around a corner, reel in at the bottom of the arc to convert the drop into speed, let go
at the apex, fire again mid-air. Reach the torii gate. Every course is new.

## Why the rope is a pivot polyline and not a joint

A `DistanceJoint2D` gets you a pendulum, but it gets you the *wrong* pendulum. The thing that makes
the Worms rope a skill mechanic is that it catches on geometry: swing past a ledge and the rope bends
around it, your radius shortens, and you suddenly accelerate. Swing back and it unwinds.

So the rope is stored as a list of pivots. Only the last one is live; everything behind it is locked
length. Three rules drive the whole thing:

1. **The constraint only pulls.** Slack rope does nothing. When the worm is further out than the free
   length, it is clamped to the circle and its *outward radial* velocity is removed. Tangential speed
   is preserved, which is what makes the swing carry.
2. **A blocked line of sight means a new pivot.** Ray from worm to active pivot every frame; if it
   hits, push a pivot at the corner and subtract that span from the free length.
3. **A flipped winding sign means a pivot pops.** Each pivot stores the sign of
   `cross(pivot - previous, worm - pivot)`. When it flips *and* the worm can see the previous pivot,
   unwind.

Reeling changes the total deployed length; the free span is the remainder after wrapped segments.

## Why levels are generated from their solution

Generating terrain and then asking "is this beatable?" needs a solver, and a solver for a swinging
game is a physics search. The cheap and total answer is to invert it: **generate the solution first,
then build a level that cannot contradict it.**

A *hop* is one intended swing — stand at `from`, fire at `anchor`, swing, land on `to`. The chain of
hops is sampled with hard constraints (both ends in rope range, anchor well above both, radii within
5 units of each other so the reel-in is reasonable). Platforms go under each landing, anchor blocks
go above each anchor point with their underside at exactly the bite point.

The **swing corridor** is the union of:

- the swing itself, modelled as a spiral (angle sweeps launch→landing bearing while the radius
  interpolates — a player reeling in mid-swing traces exactly this)
- the rope's line of sight from both ends of the hop to the anchor
- headroom above every platform, so you can always stand and aim

Every hazard and every decorative rock, pillar and stalactite is tested against that corridor and
thrown away if it intrudes. Decoration is *carved*, never negotiated.

`LevelValidator` then re-derives all of it from the finished data as an independent check, and
`LevelGenerator.Generate` re-rolls until it passes — falling back to level-0 difficulty and fresh
seeds rather than ever handing out something unvalidated.

`NinjaWormTests.SoakLevels` runs 2000 levels across the difficulty ramp as the regression test.

## Difficulty ramp

Everything scales off `t = clamp01(levelIndex / 12)`:

| | level 1 | level 13+ |
|---|---|---|
| hops | 5–7 | 12–14 |
| hop distance | 6–9 u | 8–13 u |
| platform width | 5.0 u | 2.6 u |
| hazard chance per hop | 15% | 75% |
| props | ~3 per hop | ~6 per hop |

## Scoring

- Shuriken sit on the intended line, so they reward flying the clean arc rather than detouring.
- Combo multiplies per shuriken up to ×8 and resets when you touch the ground — the incentive is to
  stay airborne, which is the incentive to keep using the rope.
- Clearing gives a time bonus and a perfect-clear bonus for every shuriken on the course.

## Juice inventory

| Trigger | Response |
|---|---|
| rope bites | hitstop 35 ms, sparks, shockwave ring, shake, chroma punch, thunk |
| rope wraps a corner | tick, sparks, small shake, rope wobble |
| shuriken | rising pitch per combo, star burst, ring, score pop, HUD punch |
| landing hard | dust scaled by impact, shake and hitstop over a threshold |
| death | hitstop 90 ms, gib burst, red flash, 0.25× slow motion, music duck |
| goal | fanfare, 0.35× slow motion, green flash, star shower |
| always | speed-driven bloom / chroma / vignette / lens distortion / saturation, camera lookahead, speed zoom, anchor lean, procedural worm whip, squash and stretch, rope sag |

`Juicer` owns `Time.timeScale`, so hitstop and slow motion layer instead of overwriting each other.

## Known gaps / next up

- Rope wrapping on very thin geometry can pop a pivot a frame late; the skin offset hides it but a
  proper corner search would be cleaner.
- No checkpoints: death restarts the level with the same seed. Fast enough that it reads as a retry.
- Level intro camera sweep, and a proper title screen.
- Music is a single four-bar loop; it is generated from a seed and could vary per level.
